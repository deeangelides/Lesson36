Meet Keith Haring

Keith Haring was an American artist whose bold, bouncing, cartoon-like figures are recognised all over the world. With just a few thick black lines and some bright colours, he drew dancing people, barking dogs and glowing babies that seem to burst with energy and joy. Haring believed that art should belong to everyone — not only to the people who visit grand galleries.

🔍 Word clue: recognised = known and remembered as soon as you see it. 👀

Drawing in the subway

In the early 1980s, Haring began making art in a very unusual place: the New York City underground railway. He noticed that some advertising boards in the subway stations were left empty and black, and he started drawing on them with white chalk. Thousands of busy travellers hurried past his lively chalk pictures every day, which turned the subway into a giant, free art gallery for the whole city.

A style all his own

Haring's style is instantly recognisable. His figures usually have no faces and almost no detail — just simple, bold outlines — and yet they are full of movement, as though they are dancing or leaping right off the wall. Around them he often drew little lines to show energy, light and action. His art looks cheerful and simple, but it was created by a very clever, hard-working artist.

Art with a message

Haring's art was not only made for fun. Through his cheerful figures, he shared important messages about love, friendship and treating everyone fairly. He wanted his art to help make the world a kinder place, and he gave a great deal of his time to helping children and good causes all around the world.

Art for everyone

To make sure ordinary people could own his art, Haring opened a shop called the Pop Shop, where his drawings were printed on t-shirts, badges and posters that anyone could afford. Keith Haring died in 1990, when he was still a young man, but his joyful, dancing figures live on — brightening walls, books and clothes all over the world.

🔍 Word clue: afford = to have enough money to buy something. 🪙

⚡ New Skill · Section 3

Oxymoron

🗣️ Say it: OK-si-MOR-on

Two words with opposite meanings, right next to each other! The clash makes a surprising, clever or funny effect.

deafening⚡silence

Silence can't really be deafening — it means it was so quiet it felt enormous. 🤫

🧭 Grammar · Section 5

Prepositions

A preposition goes before a noun or pronoun and connects it to another word. It shows where, which way or when.

📍 Where?

at · in · on · under · over · above · below · before · behind · beside · between · among · within

➡️ Which way?

into · out of · towards · away · upon · through · across · along · around · from … to

⏰ When?

before · after · during · until · by · at · on · in

🧩 Prepositional phrase = preposition + noun: across the wall, during the night. Some sentences use two: “Go down the stairs and through the door.”
